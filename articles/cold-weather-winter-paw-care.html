Cold Weather and Winter Paw Care for Dogs

By David Moreno · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Paw pads are tougher than skin, but they're not immune to winter conditions — cold surfaces, ice, and de-icing chemicals all pose real, specific risks that go beyond just discomfort. A little seasonal preparation prevents most of what actually causes problems.

What actually damages paws in winter

  • Prolonged contact with snow and ice — can cause frostbite in extreme cold, and ice buildup between toe pads is uncomfortable and can crack skin.
  • Rock salt and de-icing chemicals — many common de-icers are irritating or mildly toxic, causing chemical burns on paw pads and illness if licked off afterward.
  • Dry, cracked pads — cold air and salt both pull moisture from paw pads, similar to how cold weather dries human skin, leading to cracking that can become painful or infected.
  • Hidden ice under snow — a slip risk, especially for senior dogs or those with existing joint issues (see our guide to arthritis in dogs).

Dog boots: worth trying, with realistic expectations

Boots are the most complete protection against cold, salt, and ice, but most dogs need a genuine adjustment period — expect an awkward, high-stepping walk the first several times, which is normal and typically improves with repeated short, positive exposures rather than one long forced session. A correct fit matters: too loose and they fall off in snow, too tight and they restrict circulation or rub.

Paw balm and wax as an alternative or supplement

A protective wax or balm applied before walks creates a barrier against salt and moisture and helps prevent cracking, without the adjustment period boots require. It's generally less complete protection than boots against sharp ice or prolonged deep snow, but a practical option for dogs who won't tolerate boots or for milder conditions.

The after-walk routine that matters most

  • Rinse or wipe paws with a warm, damp cloth after every winter walk to remove salt and chemical residue before the dog can lick it off.
  • Check between toes for ice balls, which form easily in dogs with longer fur between the pads and can be genuinely painful.
  • Inspect pads for cracks, redness, or irritation, especially after walking on heavily salted sidewalks.
  • Dry paws thoroughly, since damp paw pads left in a cold environment are more prone to cracking.

Trimming paw fur helps more than people expect

For dogs with fur between the paw pads, keeping it trimmed short reduces how much snow and ice ball up there during walks — those ice balls are a common, easily preventable source of winter walk discomfort and even limping that's sometimes mistaken for a more serious injury.

Choosing pet-safe de-icer for your own property

If you use de-icing products around your own home, pet-safe formulations (typically labeled as such, avoiding the harsher traditional rock salt) reduce the risk for your own dog and any neighborhood dogs that pass by, and are worth the modest price difference for that reason alone.
